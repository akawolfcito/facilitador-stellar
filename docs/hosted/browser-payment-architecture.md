# Browser payment architecture

**Status: design only. Nothing here is implemented.**
Date: 2026-08-15. Written against `feat/hosted-testnet-facilitator` @ `43bb303`.

The question: what is the safest and simplest way to let a reviewer execute one
real x402 Stellar testnet payment from x402seek.xyz, without exposing private
keys, creating an abuse faucet, or misleading anyone about custody?

---

## 0. The hard rule

Rejected on sight, whatever else they offer:

- a private key or seed phrase field of any kind
- any UI that asks a visitor to paste a secret
- the hosted buyer or facilitator secret reaching the browser, by any route,
  including frontend env vars and `localStorage`
- a generalised payment proxy that accepts a caller-supplied `payTo`, resource,
  amount, or facilitator URL

No option below is allowed to require any of these, and none does.

## 1. What the code already establishes

Three facts were read out of the codebase rather than assumed, and they decide
most of the analysis.

**The signer is an interface, not a secret.** `@x402/stellar` exports
`ClientStellarSigner = { address: string; signAuthEntry: SignAuthEntry }`, and
`SignAuthEntry` is imported from `@stellar/stellar-sdk/contract` — the same type
SEP-43 browser wallets implement. `createEd25519Signer(secret, network)` is one
implementation of that shape, not the shape itself. A browser wallet can
therefore be handed to `new ExactStellarScheme(signer)` with no change to
`@x402/stellar`, no change to the facilitator, and no change to the seller.
This is the single most important finding in this document: option A costs far
less than it would if signing were built around a raw key.

**The live 402 is already the authority.** `apps/e2e-stellar/src/hosted-client.ts`
reads the seller's current terms from the `payment-required` header and fails
closed. The catalog is advisory and the code already treats it that way. Any
browser flow inherits this for free, provided it signs what the live 402 said
and not what the search result said.

**x402seek-web holds no secret.** It is a read-only proxy with a hard-coded
upstream host list and a catalog-derived allowlist for `inspect`. That property
is worth defending; it is the reason the site can be audited in an afternoon.

## 2. Options

### A. User-controlled Stellar wallet (SEP-43)

The visitor signs with their own testnet account through a browser wallet.

**Security.** The best of the four. No key ever reaches our servers or our
JavaScript. Server compromise cannot move the visitor's funds. Browser
compromise is the visitor's own wallet's threat model, not ours. Custody is
unambiguous and needs no disclaimer.

**UX.** The worst of the four, and the gap is not close. Install a wallet,
switch to testnet, then acquire canonical testnet USDC
(`CBIELTK6YBZJU5UP2WWQEUCYKLPU6AUNZ2BQ4WWFEIE3USCIHMXQDAMA`), which on Stellar
means establishing the asset and finding a source for it. XLM is not a problem —
fee sponsorship means the buyer needs none, which is one of the nicer things
this project can demonstrate — but USDC is. A reviewer with fifteen minutes will
not finish.

**Stellar compatibility.** Classic `G…` accounts only, which is what the
facilitator supports today and what a SEP-43 wallet produces by default. Contract
accounts remain unsupported, and that limitation does not bite here. Fee
sponsorship works unchanged: the facilitator sponsors regardless of who signed.

**x402 compatibility.** Complete, and this is the option's real strength. The
existing client helpers are reused as they stand. Verify and settle are
untouched. The live 402 stays authoritative.

**Operations.** Nothing new. No secret, no funding, no account lifecycle, no
budget to monitor. The cheapest option to run by a wide margin.

**Abuse.** None that is ours. A visitor can only spend their own money.

**Effort.** MEDIUM. Wallet detection and connection, a signer adapter over the
wallet's `signAuthEntry`, and error paths for wrong network, missing trustline
and insufficient balance. The adapter itself is small precisely because the
interface already fits.

**Reviewer value.** High for the small number who arrive already holding testnet
USDC. Near zero for everyone else, because they will stop at the funding step.

**Verdict: viable, valuable, and not sufficient on its own.**

### B. Ephemeral browser wallet

The page generates a fresh testnet keypair for the session.

**Security.** Weaker than it looks. The key is generated by our JavaScript and
lives in the tab. Calling it "user-controlled" is a claim the architecture does
not support: whoever controls the page controls the key. It is not a
catastrophe, because the key holds nothing but testnet dust, but it is a custody
statement we would be making loosely.

**UX.** Good until the funding step, then identical to option A: the fresh
account has no USDC.

**Abuse.** This is where it fails. The only way to make the ephemeral wallet
useful is for us to fund it, which means an endpoint that sends testnet USDC to
an arbitrary caller-supplied address. That is a faucet. Faucets get drained,
scripted, and used as free liquidity for unrelated testing, and defending one is
a larger project than the payment demo it exists to support.

**Effort.** MEDIUM to HIGH once the faucet and its defences are counted.

**Reviewer value.** Same as option C, at strictly higher risk.

**Verdict: reject.** It does not solve the funding problem, it relocates it into
the shape hardest to defend, and it buys a custody story that is not true.

### C. Server-side demo buyer

A dedicated, tightly bounded testnet buyer account executes one demo payment on
the visitor's behalf.

**Security.** Requires holding a buyer secret, which x402seek-web deliberately
does not do today. That property is preserved by putting the demo buyer in its
own service, alongside the facilitator and the seller, and letting the web app
proxy to it exactly as it already proxies discovery. The web app stays
secret-free; a new service holds one testnet secret worth a fraction of a cent.
Blast radius of compromise is the demo buyer's float, and nothing else — it is
not the facilitator signer and must never be.

The honesty requirement is real and easy to meet: the UI has to say that
x402Seek's demo buyer paid, not the visitor. Anything vaguer misleads about
custody, which is the one failure this whole exercise is meant to avoid.

**UX.** The best of the four by a wide margin. One click, no wallet, no
extension, no funding, no trustline. A reviewer sees a real settlement on
Stellar testnet within seconds of arriving.

**Stellar and x402 compatibility.** Identical to what already runs. This is the
hosted client flow, already proven end to end in `HOSTED-E-01…06`, moved behind
a button. Fee sponsorship, classic accounts, canonical USDC, verify and settle
all stay exactly as they are.

**Operations.** The most demanding option. A new secret, a funded USDC float, a
spend ceiling to monitor, rate limits to tune, and an account whose balance
someone has to top up. The facilitator's existing metrics and limiter give a
pattern to copy rather than invent.

**Abuse.** The question that decides whether this ships. Bounded by
construction, not by good behaviour:

| Bound | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Resource allowlist | exactly one: the hosted seller's `/summarize` |
| Seller allowlist | exactly one `payTo`, the hosted seller address |
| Asset | canonical testnet USDC contract, pinned |
| Maximum amount | 10000 base units (0.001 USDC), rejected above |
| Network | `stellar:testnet`, locked, pubnet unreachable |
| Per-IP rate | a small number per hour, on the /64 for IPv6 |
| Global rate | a small number per minute across all callers |
| Daily spend ceiling | fixed; the route returns 429 once reached, and says so |
| Buyer float | funded to a known small amount, alerted below a floor |
| Caller-supplied input | none: no `payTo`, no amount, no resource, no facilitator URL |

With no caller-supplied destination there is no proxy and no SSRF surface. With
a fixed amount and a fixed recipient, the worst an anonymous attacker achieves
is exhausting a day's demo budget, which costs testnet USDC and some sponsored
XLM. That is an availability annoyance, not a loss.

**Effort.** MEDIUM. One new service reusing `hosted-client.ts` almost verbatim,
one new proxied route in the web app, one button and one result panel.

**Reviewer value.** Highest. It converts "here is a transaction hash from a run
we did" into "here is a transaction hash from the run you just did".

**Verdict: viable, and the only option that reaches a reviewer who has no
Stellar setup.**

### D. No browser payment

Keep the browser read-only: seek, inspect the live 402, read the recorded
evidence, run the developer client if you want to pay.

**Security.** Perfect, trivially. **Operations.** Nothing. **Effort.** Zero.

**What is lost.** Less than it first appears. A reviewer can already watch
semantic discovery answer and abstain live, read the seller's current 402 terms
decoded from the live header, and follow a real settlement to Stellar Expert.
What is missing is the last link: they cannot cause a payment themselves, so the
settlement stays something they are told about rather than something they
witnessed. For a proposal whose entire argument is "we settled this, here is the
hash", that gap is worth closing.

**Verdict: acceptable, and the correct fallback if option C's controls cannot
be met in the time available.**

## 3. Threat model

Applies to option C, since it is the recommendation. Marked ✅ where a control
already exists in the deployed system and ➕ where it would be new.

| Threat | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Key theft from the browser | ✅ no key in the browser, in any option |
| Key theft from the server | ➕ demo buyer isolated from the facilitator signer; separate service, separate secret, minimal float |
| Unauthorised payment | ➕ no caller-supplied `payTo`, resource or amount; the route takes no payment parameters at all |
| Arbitrary seller abuse | ➕ single-resource allowlist keyed on the canonical resource URL |
| SSRF / proxy abuse | ✅ pattern already in `src/live.ts`: hard-coded hosts, catalog-derived allowlist, no caller-supplied URL |
| USDC drain | ➕ per-IP and global rate limits, daily spend ceiling, small float |
| Fee (XLM) drain | ✅ sponsorship is already metered by the facilitator; ➕ the same ceiling bounds it |
| Replay | ✅ Soroban auth entries carry a nonce and an expiry ledger; a replayed authorisation fails on chain |
| Duplicate clicks | ➕ disable on submit plus a short server-side idempotency window per IP |
| Stale catalog terms | ✅ the flow must fetch the live 402 and sign that; already how `hosted-client.ts` works |
| Malformed live 402 | ✅ fails closed today; ➕ surface the refusal in the UI rather than a generic error |
| Ambiguous settlement response | ✅ known limitation: `SettleResponse` carries no fee field, so fee reporting stays PARTIAL and must not be presented as exact |

## 4. Recommendation

**Primary: C, the server-side demo buyer, as its own service.**

Because it is the only option that reaches a reviewer who has no Stellar setup,
because its risk is bounded by construction rather than by trust, and because it
is the most faithful to what x402Seek claims. The thesis is that a program
decides what is worth paying for and pays for it autonomously. A demo buyer
doing exactly that, under a spend ceiling, is the product, not a shortcut around
it. What it must never be is vague about who paid.

**Second: A, the SEP-43 wallet**, offered alongside rather than instead. It
costs little now that the signer is known to be a plain interface, it carries no
custody question at all, and it is the honest path for anyone who already holds
testnet USDC. Ship it after C, not before.

**Reject: B.** It moves the funding problem into a faucet and claims a custody
story the architecture does not support.

**D remains the fallback**, and remains a defensible place to stand.

### Required new security controls

Resource allowlist; seller `payTo` allowlist; pinned asset; maximum amount;
network lock; per-IP and global rate limits; daily spend ceiling with an
explicit 429; idempotency window; float floor alert; strict separation of the
demo buyer secret from the facilitator signer.

### Required new infrastructure

One Railway service; one funded testnet buyer account (USDC float plus a small
XLM reserve, though sponsorship means fees are covered); one secret in Railway,
never in the web app; a spend counter that survives restart; one proxied
read-only route in x402seek-web.

### Expected size

Roughly: one new app (config, app, buyer route, limiter, spend ledger, tests),
one new proxy route plus its tests in x402seek-web, one button and one result
panel in the UI. Comparable to `apps/demo-seller`, which came in around the same
shape.

### SCF value

It closes the last gap between the claim and the reviewer's own experience. The
proposal currently says a payment happened and offers a hash. This lets the
reviewer cause one and read their own hash on Stellar Expert, which is a
different kind of evidence.

## 5. Not decided here

Whether to build it at all is the user's call, and the exact wording that keeps
custody unambiguous should be settled before any code, not after.
